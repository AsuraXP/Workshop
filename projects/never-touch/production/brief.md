# Production Brief — Why You Never Actually Touch Anything

- **Title:** Why You Never Actually Touch Anything
- **Input Mode:** Autonomous prompt -> Crew-authored script + synthesized dual-voice narration & character dialogue (`voice-00` Narrator, `voice-01` Nora)
- **Genre & Tone:** Curiosity physics explainer with dry character comedy and warm human payoff
- **Aspect & Runtime:** `16:9` (`1920x1080`) @ `24 fps` · `66.0s` (`12` shots)
- **Visual Style:** `awaken` (`bars: "none"`, full-bleed twilight/warm/daylight/blueprint staging)
- **Core Premise:** Because every atom is surrounded by negatively charged electron shells (governed by Coulomb repulsion and the Pauli exclusion principle), solid objects stop ~`0.1–0.3 nm` apart; what our nerves perceive as "touch" is electromagnetic force—yet that tiny gap is what keeps us from nuclear fusion while still transmitting warmth, weight, and human connection.
- **Motif:** The glowing `0.2 nm` amber/cyan forcefield gap—introduced as an unsettling paradox at a study table, tested as a joke on a park bench, feared in a fusion chamber, and celebrated in a warm mug clink & handshake at dusk.
